# Example Research Profile

This is a generic example to help you create your own profile. Copy it, rename it, and fill in your specific research interests.

## Profile

- **Profile name:** my-research-topic
- **Core topics:** (your main research keywords — papers matching these are highest priority)
- **Secondary topics:** (adjacent themes that are useful but not your primary focus)
- **Study systems or organisms:** (what systems do you study? e.g., forests, wetlands, urban areas, coral reefs)
- **Ecosystems or regions:** (geographic focus, e.g., temperate forests, tropical regions, specific countries)
- **Methods or data types:** (what methods matter to you? e.g., remote sensing, field experiments, meta-analysis, GIS)
- **Exclusion topics:** (what should be filtered out? e.g., purely clinical studies, business/management papers)
- **Must-track journals:** (journals you always want to see)
- **Nice-to-track journals:** (journals you want to see when papers are relevant)
- **Weekly reading capacity:** (how many papers can you realistically read per week?)
- **Reminder threshold in days:** (after how many days should unread high-priority papers be flagged?)
- **Preferred summary language:** (English, Chinese, etc.)

## Keyword tiers

These keywords drive automatic paper screening. The screen_by_profile.py script uses them to classify papers into tiers.

### Core keywords

Papers matching these are classified as **core** — directly on-topic.

```text
keyword-1
keyword-2
keyword-3
```

### Proxy keywords

Papers matching these are classified as **proxy** — adjacent methods or themes.

```text
proxy-keyword-1
proxy-keyword-2
```

### Eco-context keywords

Papers matching these are classified as **eco** — broader ecological context.

```text
eco-keyword-1
eco-keyword-2
```

## Example: Housing Policy and Spatial Mismatch

```text
Profile name: housing-policy-skill-mismatch
Core topics: affordable housing, housing vouchers, spatial mismatch, skill mismatch, labor mobility, urban productivity
Secondary topics: place-based policy, housing subsidies, inter-city migration, human capital externalities
Study systems or organisms: cities, metropolitan areas, urban labor markets
Ecosystems or regions: United States, China, Europe
Methods or data types: causal inference (RDD, DID, IV), administrative data, census/ACS micro-data, spatial econometrics
Exclusion topics: pure corporate finance, clinical health studies, purely theoretical models without empirical content
Must-track journals: Journal of Urban Economics, Journal of Public Economics, American Economic Journal: Economic Policy
Nice-to-track journals: Journal of Regional Science, Land Economics, Journal of Development Economics
Weekly reading capacity: 6 papers
Reminder threshold in days: 10
Preferred summary language: English or Chinese
```

## Example: Fiscal Competition and Public Goods

```text
Profile name: fiscal-competition-public-goods
Core topics: inter-jurisdictional competition, public goods provision, fiscal decentralization, tax competition, spillovers
Secondary topics: political economy of public finance, local government debt, infrastructure investment
Study systems or organisms: city governments, provincial governments, local authorities
Ecosystems or regions: China, United States, OECD countries
Methods or data types: panel data, spatial regression, instrumental variables, natural experiments
Exclusion topics: purely theoretical public finance without empirical identification, clinical studies
Must-track journals: Journal of Public Economics, Journal of Urban Economics, Journal of Comparative Economics
Nice-to-track journals: European Economic Review, Public Administration Review, Government Information Quarterly
Weekly reading capacity: 5 papers
Reminder threshold in days: 10
Preferred summary language: English or Chinese
```
